## [Unreleased]

### Fixed
- 2026-08-02 — **The bright line along the horizon: the cloud upsample renormalised its own rejection away.** `cloud_composite.frag`'s depth-aware upsample demotes a mismatched bilinear tap to weight `0.05` rather than `0`. That works while at least one tap is still `1.0`. When **all four** are rejected they are all `0.05`, and dividing by their sum renormalises them straight back into ordinary bilinear weights — the rejection cancels itself out and the filter blends across the largest discontinuity in the buffer. The `wsum < 1e-6` guard could never catch it, because `4 × 0.05` is not a small number.
  - All four fail on exactly one row of ground pixels: the epsilon is 2 % *relative* depth, and at grazing incidence one screen row spans an unbounded range of ground distance. The band stays one row wide at any camera height or field of view, because bilinear reaches only one texel — every row below it has all four taps inside the clean ground region, where the filter is a no-op.
  - It read near-*white* rather than as a soft seam because of what the blend does to the **distance**: it drags `cloud_distance` from ~100 km down under `AERIAL_MAX_DISTANCE`, so that row skips the entire long-path extinction tail. A convex blend of two neighbours can never be brighter than both; skipping the extinction can.
  - Fixed by taking the single nearest-depth tap outright when nothing matches. Found by an adversarially-verified audit in which the leading hypothesis — the editor grid plane bounding the march's `t1` — was **refuted**: `GridPass` is a fullscreen pass that writes no depth, ray-casts the reference ellipsoid rather than a flat plane, and runs *after* the clouds.
- 2026-08-02 — **Auto-exposure could not reach a physically lit night, and its control reads backwards.** `exposure = key / exp2(clamp(avg_log, min_ev, max_ev))`, so `min_ev` clamps the metered average from below and therefore the exposure from *above* — it is the **maximum-exposure** control, and lowering it *brightens* dark scenes. The engine's night is physically exact (the ephemeris derives starlight at 8.3e-9 of sunlight and full moonlight at ~2.4e-6, both within ~20 % of the measured lux ratios), which puts a moonlit deck near 1e-5 in scene units and needs ~1000× of gain to read as grey. The old default of −6 capped the gain at 11.5× and the editor's slider stopped at −10 (184×), so a correct night was not merely mistuned but **unreachable from the UI**.
  - Default `min_ev` −6 → −14; slider range extended to −16; both fields relabelled by what they do (`Darkest Metered` / `Brightest Metered`) with hints stating the inverse relationship, and the misleading `EV` unit dropped — these are log2 of luminance in the renderer's own units, not photographic EV100.
  - Documented that `adapt()`'s `max(avg_luminance, 1e-4)` divide-guard is also a hard 1800× exposure ceiling, so `min_ev` stops doing anything below about −13.3.
- 2026-08-02 — **Terrain's first drawn frame lost the device**, and the cause is a class of bug the push-descriptor design makes easy to write. Set 0 is a *push* set, `pbr.frag` reads twenty-six descriptors out of it, and the terrain pass wrote the two its own vertex shader wanted. The other twenty-four were undefined, the fragment shader sampled them regardless, and `vkQueueSubmit2` returned `VK_ERROR_DEVICE_LOST` the moment the camera reached the Moon. Terrain also pushed material index 0 without ever pushing a material, so `pbr.frag` read whatever those bytes held — bindless texture indices among them — which is the same failure by a second route.
  - Fixed structurally rather than with a third copy. `passes/shading_set.hpp` now owns the set as a pair that must stay paired: `declare_shading_set` (the graph reads that make it safe to read) and `write_shading_set` (the descriptors themselves). The opaque and transparent passes carried identical copies of both; all three call one now. A new binding reaches every shading pass instead of two of three, and a fourth shading pass is a line rather than a debugging session.
  - Terrain pushes one material per frame, coloured from `PlanetParams::ground_albedo` — the colour the analytic ground would have used — so handing the body over to real geometry changes what shades it without changing what it looks like.
- 2026-08-02 — **The Assembly window crashed on open**, and the cause is worth recording because it is a class of bug rather than one. `scalar_field` and `vector3_field` open with `ImGui::TableNextRow` — they are *row emitters* for the two-column table the Inspector's Transform block sets up — and their doc said so, as a description of what they do rather than as a precondition on where they may be called. Calling one from a panel that draws no table dereferences a null current table: an access violation, not a layout glitch.
  - Fixed by making the widgets **total** rather than by teaching every caller a rule. They now lay themselves out according to where they find themselves — a table row inside a table, a plain labelled drag outside one — so there is no precondition left to forget. The table form is untouched, so the Inspector is unchanged. A widget that works in one context and crashes in another is a trap whose only defence is documentation nobody re-reads.
  - The Assembly and joint panels also stopped reaching for those two entirely and draw plain rows, which is what `ComponentEditor::number` already does for every component section and was the pattern to follow in the first place.
- 2026-08-02 — `cooked_asset_extension` had no case for `CookedAssetKind::NodeBeam`, so a node-beam asset written through the store landed as `.sushicooked`. The build had been warning about the unhandled enumerator on every editor compile.
- 2026-08-02 — **Clouds received no skylight and no moonlight.** Gating the sun at the horizon (below) was right for the direct beam and wrong for the scene: the deck went perfectly black after sunset, when a cloudy evening is grey and legible and a moonlit one plainly lit. The `sun_radiance * 0.02` ambient proxy that had been standing in for skylight had no colour of its own and, once the beam was correctly gated, went to exactly zero — taking the whole night sky's illumination with it.
  - `cloud.frag` now binds `SKY_VIEW_LUT_BINDING` and takes two taps per pixel — the zenith and the sun's azimuth at the horizon — which bracket what a deck actually receives: the zenith's blue at midday, and the whole of a twilight in the minutes after sunset. Scaled by the **raw** top-of-atmosphere beam rather than the attenuated one, because the LUT stores in-scatter per unit TOA radiance and carries the atmospheric path internally (`sky.frag:557` scales the identical fetch the same way); using the attenuated beam would apply the atmosphere twice.
  - The Moon and every other reflecting body now reach the clouds. `Astro::celestial_lights` already derives each as a real directional light from its albedo, radius, distance and phase — sorted by what it *delivers*, so after sunset `lights[0]` is the Moon rather than the Sun, and a crescent is dimmer than a full moon with no authored difference. None of it had ever been read by the cloud pass. `cloud.frag` skips the emitters (the Sun is the direct beam), gates each body at its own horizon and attenuates it through the transmittance LUT.
  - Folded into the ambient rather than marched as a second self-shadowed beam, which is a physical choice and not a shortcut: these sources are five to six orders of magnitude under the sun, so what survives to the eye is the multiply-scattered part, and that leaves an optically thick deck near-uniformly grey. It is also what the sun's light volume could not have helped with — that volume is baked toward the sun, so below the horizon its depths describe a ray travelling *down* out of the deck and would shadow moonlight with geometry that has nothing to do with where the Moon is. Artistic control stays `NightLighting::reflected_intensity`, which the ephemeris already applies; there is deliberately no second knob in the shader.
- 2026-08-02 — **Clouds were lit by a sun that never set.** `cloud.frag` multiplied `scene.sun_color * scene.sun_dir.w` — the *top of atmosphere* beam — and applied no atmospheric transmittance and no horizon test, so the deck was lit at full noon radiance at every hour, including after dark. The ephemeris only writes the sun's *direction*; its radiance is an authored constant that nothing dims. Every other sun consumer attenuates the beam for itself (`sky.frag`, `fog_scatter.comp`, `aerial_perspective.comp`, `sky_view_lut.comp`, `multiscatter_lut.comp` all sample the transmittance LUT); the cloud march was the only one that did not. Result: a black evening sky with a snow-white deck in front of it, and no sunset colour anywhere on a cloud.
  - `CloudPass` now takes `AtmosphereLutPass` and writes `TRANSMITTANCE_LUT_BINDING` the way `SkyPass` does. `cloud.frag` builds the same `AtmosphereMedium` and attenuates `sun_radiance` once per pixel at the deck's mid-altitude — the sun's slant path varies by well under a per cent across a 1–2 km deck, so a per-sample fetch on up to 160 marched samples would buy nothing.
  - Added the missing horizon gate (`atmo_ray_sphere(...) > 0` ⇒ no direct beam). Without it the LUT's parameterisation folds back below the horizon and returns the *upward* answer, which is what kept the deck lit through the night.
  - This also fixes the `ambient_color = scene.ambient + sun_radiance * 0.02` leak for free: with the beam pinned at noon, that proxy was the *dominant* ambient component after dark (the ephemeris-driven `scene.ambient` correctly collapses to a starlight floor), so even a fully self-shadowed cloud face kept a flat colourless glow. It now dies with the sun and reddens through twilight.
- 2026-08-02 — **Distant clouds had no aerial perspective past 31 km.** The froxel volume's last slice sits at `AERIAL_MAX_DISTANCE`, but a deck seen from the ground runs out to its own base-sphere tangent — over 100 km — so the entire horizon strip was hazed as though it were 31 km away and stopped darkening with distance altogether. That is most of the solid white band along the horizon.
  - `cloud_composite.frag` continues the view path analytically past the volume using Bruneton's ratio identity on the transmittance LUT (`T(near→far) = T(near→top) / T(far→top)`, two fetches, paid only by pixels past 31 km). The achromatic part folds into `aerial.a`; the chromatic residual is applied to the cloud directly, because reddening a distant deck is exactly what the volume's single luma-scalar throughput channel had no room to carry.
  - The composite's additive in-scatter was also missing the `sun_radiance` scale that `sky.frag` applies to the identical fetch, so it arrived at 1/20th strength (the authored sun is 20). Distant cloud was therefore only ever *dimmed* by a grey factor and never pushed toward the sky's colour — the half of aerial perspective that replaces an object with haze was effectively absent.
- 2026-08-02 — **The GPU profiler stopped measuring after 16 passes, and steered auto-exposure with the truncated sum.** `MAX_TIMED_PASSES` was 16 against ~44 registered passes; `GpuProfiler::begin_pass` returns `INVALID_TIMER` once the budget is spent, so overflow read as *absence* — at Ultra, where more tier-gated passes are enabled ahead of them, `sky` and `clouds` vanished from the panel and looked like passes that were not running. `measured_frame_milliseconds()` is the sum of those timings and drives both the auto-exposure adaptation rate and the dynamic-resolution governor, so both were being steered by a number that omitted most of the frame. Raised to 64.
- 2026-08-02 — **Every body but Earth had its surface rotated, by up to 117°.** `body_rotation_angle` returns the IAU prime-meridian angle W, which is measured from the ascending node of the body's equator on the **J2000 equator**; `ecliptic_to_body_equatorial` builds a frame whose +X is the node with the **ecliptic**. Both are valid frames, they are not the same frame, and the engine was adding W to a longitude expressed in the second one — a category error that silently turns a body's whole surface. The gap is a fixed property of each pole and it is large: **52.68° for the Moon, −40.86° for Mars, −117.65° for Venus**. Earth had the same class of mismatch at 0.31° (35 km at the equator), because the sky basis used sidereal time while the body-fixed conversion used W.
  - Nothing had noticed because nothing else spoke this frame. Every existing test was a round trip through the same wrong convention, and a uniformly rotated planet with a uniformly rotated sky is self-consistent. Real elevation data is what made it observable, because a map has opinions about where Mare Serenitatis is.
  - `Astro::prime_meridian_angle(body, jd)` is now the single answer to "where is the prime meridian right now", and the four consumers go through it: `fill_environment_sky`, `scene_frame_for`, and both directions of the simulation's body-fixed pose conversion. Earth resolves to Greenwich sidereal time — the quantity the home sky has always used — so the home sky does not move, and its body-fixed conversion picks up the 0.31° as a fix. Not bit-identical, and measured rather than claimed: the meridian is now wrapped before the longitude is added instead of after, which is the same angle modulo a turn but not the same double. Over 4000 epochs and 37 places the worst direction moves **6.3 × 10⁻¹⁶ rad — 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ arcseconds, four nanometres at Earth's radius**.
  - The proof is deliberately **external to the conversion**, since self-consistency proves nothing here. The Moon is tidally locked, so from selenographic (0, 0) the Earth must stand within the libration cone of the zenith, about 10°. Before: 29°–40° of elevation. After: **80°–89°**. All 63 existing astro tests pass unchanged; 7 new ones pin the offsets, the tidal-lock check, and that an observer's zenith reads back their own coordinates to 1e-9°.
- 2026-08-02 — **Terrain slot eviction could overwrite an image a frame in flight was still reading.** `TileResidency::insert` refused to evict slots bound *this* frame, which is right for a caller that submits and waits and wrong for a three-deep frame chain: frames N−1 and N−2 hold bindings the device has not finished with, and the render graph cannot help because the write and the read are in different submissions. Eviction now spares a configurable window of frames, which `TileCache` sets to its staging ring depth; a window of one is exactly the old behaviour, so the callers that wanted it still get it.
- 2026-08-01 — **Cloud shape: the carve had the wrong sign.** Every version of the carve until now built clouds by *subtraction* — threshold a base field, remove material where an erosion noise is high — which can only produce concave features, because removing material from a solid is how you dig. A cumulus congestus top is the opposite: convex protrusions bulging outward, with the readability living in the creases between them. This is why no setting of the erosion strength ever produced cauliflower, only smooth lumps or shredded filaments.
  - `cloud.frag` gained a **billow ladder** (three inverted-Worley octaves) **added to the base field before the threshold**, displacing the isosurface outward into a bulge instead of biting a hollow out of a finished shape. Zero-mean so the envelope's coverage still arrives, clamped at 0.35 base-units so a wrong amplitude estimate bounds turret size rather than swallowing the threshold. Band-limits itself per octave against the march footprint.
  - The polarity was already free: `noise_worley` returns `1 − distance`, so channel `g` is high at cell centres and low at cell walls. The old code took `1 − g` and subtracted it, converting round bumps into round *holes*.
  - `cloud_noise_volume.comp` kind 4's `g` channel was an fbm of an fbm, nine octaves deep; each nesting multiplies the standard deviation by 0.68, leaving a field too flat to displace anything visible and burying the crisp cell boundaries that are the whole point. Now nearly a single inverted Worley octave (frequencies 4/9/19, mutually incommensurate) — the march's ladder supplies the fbm.
  - **Creases are now shaded, not merely carved.** The ladder's signed value doubles as a local occlusion measure and feeds extra sun-depth plus an ambient factor. Without it the cauliflower is geometrically present and shades as flat white, because the light volume's 128 m texels cannot resolve a 100 m crease.
  - Subtraction survives only under the convective base, where a cloud really is being torn, weighted by `1 − cauliflower` so the regimes never fight over a sample. Its polarity is corrected too: the old `wisp = g` removed cell *interiors* and left their walls — a web of thin ridges, and the strand generator all along.
- 2026-08-01 — **Cloud march step policy** (`cloud.frag`): the march took roughly **one sample per cloud** and never integrated the sky immediately in front of the camera. One constant caused both — `seg_min = min(march_len / STEPS, max(shell_thick * 0.12, 40))`, twelve per cent of the *union* of every enabled deck, which is 1344 m in the default 800–12 000 m sky. Because it was also the clamp's lower bound, the constant-angular rule never engaged until 54 km out, so a 1.5 km cumulus was integrated as a single slab. The near-camera hole tracked genus rather than distance — absent with stratocumulus alone (thin union, ~100 m band), 1.3 km with any tall deck enabled — which is what identified the cause.
  - Searching for cloud and integrating one are now separate rules. Integration is angular (2 % of camera distance, floored at 20 m, ceilinged at 1200 m); the interval being integrated is tracked separately from the jittered sample inside it, an unbiased midpoint rule that starts at `t0` exactly and leaves no uncovered span. The old code sampled at the interval's left endpoint from a jittered start, so `seg_min * dither` of every ray was integrated by nothing.
  - `cloud_primary_steps_far` rescaled from "minimum step count across the march length" — which made the same tier resolve a horizon ray at 5 km per sample and a vertical ray at 500 m — to a scale-free multiplier on the angular rate, so one tier means one sampling density in every direction. Shipped tiers land at 0.040 / 0.027 / 0.020 / 0.013.
  - Empty-space skipping is now conservative. `t += max(dist_step, cell_size)` against a 512 m near cell hopped 2.6 untested cells per probe on a near-horizontal ray; `cloud_skip_exit` computes the exact distance to the boundary of the region the *trilinear* probe proved empty (dual-lattice DDA across both windows, hence the half-texel offset), so the hop is maximally long and never crosses unexamined sky. `cloud_probe`'s vertical early-out gained a one-metre margin because the first probe of every ray sits exactly on the base sphere, where float32 at planetary radius resolves to half a metre and the answer was a coin flip no texel had been asked for.
  - `cloud_field_window.glsl` gained `CLOUD_SKIP_RESOLUTION_Y` / `CLOUD_FAR_RESOLUTION_Y`, mirroring `CloudscapeCompilePass::FIELD_RESOLUTION_Y` and `SKIP_DOWNSAMPLE_Y` (both `cloud_field_params` and `cloud_field_pattern` are full, and these are fixed engine sizes rather than tiered settings — the same coupling `cloudscape_skip.comp` already declares). The reciprocal note is on the C++ constants.

### Changed
- 2026-08-01 — **CloudsV2**: rebuilt the volumetric cloud visual model around a per-sample analytic carve (the War Thunder / Nubis arrangement), replacing the bake-time carve whose 128 m / 1024 m window texels could not hold a cloud's shape and whose march-side detail only existed within 200 m of the camera — the structural cause of the "smeared cotton" verdict. Design record: `docs/slop/atmosphere_system.md` §7.6.
  - `cloudscape_field.comp` stores only the physics **envelope** now: r = coverage (nest cloud fraction top-openness-tapered, or deck coverage height-gradient-tapered), g = vertical profile, a = in-cloud water at half scale. All noise taps left the bake; decks combine as a coverage union instead of a density sum.
  - `cloud.frag` carves the shape per march sample at **every distance**: domain warp, CDF-uniformised base threshold at `1 − envelope` (coverage-adaptive 1/√envelope scale, floored at 4× the local step — the old Nyquist floor restated against the true sampler), height-flipped Worley erosion fading over 14–42 km, a fine octave and curl warp near the camera, and a faded hand-off to the statistical mean past 80 km. The carve runs in the bake's own world-anchored pattern frame, published as `cloud_field_pattern` through the scene tail by `CloudscapeCompilePass`.
  - New precombined march noise volume (`cloud_noise_volume.comp` kind 4, `CloudNoise::march`, 128³): uniformised base / erosion fbm / fine fbm / curl potential in one texture, because the march has exactly one free image binding.
  - Every sun-depth integral (light volume, far light channel, cloud shadow map, panorama, inline cone march) states mass against the carved sky via the shared `CLOUD_ENVELOPE_MEAN_SHAPE = 0.45`; the skip field pools the unscaled envelope×water product so the empty-space probe stays a conservative ceiling.
  - The far bake's supersampling dropped 8×8 → 2×2: it anti-aliased a threshold that no longer exists in the bake.
- 2026-08-01 — Fixed the cloud march's horizon reach (the "clouds exist only in a camera-following bubble" defect, confirmed by eye from 15 km): the march length was capped at fourteen *shell thicknesses*, calibrated when the shell was the genus catalogue's 10+ km union (~150 km); the nest publishes the measured condensate span, and fourteen times a 1.3 km fair-weather deck is 19 km — the far window was baked, addressed, lit, and never marched. The cap is an absolute 160 km now (cloud.frag and the panorama impostor both). Two step-rule repairs ride along, without which the longer march would starve: the near step floor is stated against the shell instead of `march_len / steps` (a 160 km horizon ray no longer puts kilometre steps through the cumulus next to the camera), and the step growth is a constant 2.5 % of camera distance (constant screen-space footprint) replacing `0.08*sqrt(metres)`, which never escaped the clamp floor and spent the whole sample budget within a dozen kilometres.

### Added
- 2026-08-02 — **The vehicle's shell is drawn.** A car reached a scene and moved and was invisible: the entity followed the rigid core and nothing rendered the body. `IVehicleService::vehicle_surface` publishes the shell's collision surface as a deformable mesh, on the same channel cloth and soft bodies already use — because how a surface's triangles were produced is not something the renderer can see (P6-G2's argument, reused). Read straight off the live node bodies with no cache in between, so a dented panel is dented on screen in the tick it dented and the drawing cannot disagree with the collision, being the same triangles.
- 2026-08-02 — **The car can be driven with the keyboard.** Arrow keys for throttle, brake and steering, Left Shift for the clutch, Page Up/Down for gears, on their own rebindable input context that is a no-op unless the selection carries a Vehicle. Arrows rather than WASD because W/E/R are the gizmo keys and a driving binding that stole them would break the tool keys the moment an author selected a car.
  - The controls are **ramped, not switched**: a key is a bit and a throttle is not, and stepping a pedal between 0 and 1 is what makes keyboard driving in a physically simulated car undriveable — every input a shock through a drivetrain that models its own inertia. Throttle, steering and their return rates all differ, because the mechanisms do. The ramp lives in the editor's input layer rather than in the physics: it is a property of the *device*, and a wheel-and-pedal set would deliver the same `VehicleInput` with none, which the vehicle must not be able to detect.
- 2026-08-02 — **`docs/VEHICLES.md`**: mesh to driving car, and what every number in the way means — what the cooker derives from a `SoftBodyMaterial`, why `core_mass_fraction` is a dial rather than a switch, why the drivetrain is not made of constraints, and the three things that are still missing, named rather than left to be discovered.
- 2026-08-02 — **The physics sample scene ships as a file.** `examples/physics_sample_scene.cpp` writes it to a `.sushiscene`, generated from the same builder the integration suite steps and asserts on — a hand-authored blob would be a second definition of the scene, free to drift from the tested one, and the drift would surface as a demo that quietly stopped demonstrating something. Removing the menu item without providing this first was the wrong order and is corrected here.
  - Building it found a latent break: the builder took an `EditorContext`, which drags ImGui in behind it and made a *scene builder* impossible to use from anything that is not the editor shell — including the test target that had just started compiling it. It takes a world and nothing else now. Recording undo and moving a selection are the caller's business.
- 2026-08-02 — **Planetary terrain reached the frame (P2b).** Every piece of the vertical slice existed and compiled; what it lacked was the one rotation that says where a body's own frame sits in the scene, which is now `Environment::planet_body_axes` alongside `planet_body`. `TerrainPass` is registered after the opaque pass, `PlanetTerrain` follows the environment's dominant body, and one instanced draw of a shared 33×33 lattice puts every visible node of the Moon on screen through `pbr.frag` — the same lighting, shadowing and tone mapping every mesh gets, because `terrain.vert` matches `mesh.vert`'s output signature exactly. Design record: `docs/slop/solar_system_overhaul.md` §9.4.
  - **The analytic ground stands down once per frame, not once per pixel.** `sky.frag` already loses the ellipsoid to anything nearer, which would have been enough if terrain were never *below* the reference surface — but every lunar mare is two kilometres below it, and there the sphere is the nearer surface and would be drawn over the basin inside it. So when the selection produced nodes, `Scene::suppress_analytic_ground` switches the body's analytic ground off for the frame. Sound because §7.1's cut is an exact cover: the quadtree always spans the whole body, coarsely at the horizon but never with a hole. A body with no pack keeps the analytic ground, unchanged.
  - **Nothing is allocated until a planet is looked at.** `PlanetTerrain` now starts with no body and no slot pool; the pool is created with the first pack that loads and kept across bodies, since the slots are anonymous storage and only their index is body-specific. A scene with no planet in it pays nothing.
  - `terrain_frame.hpp` carries the camera and its frustum across the boundary, header-only and Vulkan-free on purpose: three conventions meet in that function — column order, which half-space a frustum plane keeps, whether the body offset is taken before or after the rotation — and each is wrong in a way that still draws a planet, so all three are checked without a device.
  - **It draws; it does not yet meet its own exit criteria, and §20.1 of the design record says exactly why.** Three things, only one of which is shading: the scene origin sits on the *reference ellipsoid* and nothing yet resolves the observer's altitude against the height field, so the camera starts inside the shell (and `VK_CULL_MODE_NONE`, right for bring-up, turns that into a skin that follows the view instead of into nothing); the shipped lunar tier stores depths 0–3, which is 2665 m per texel, so there is no measured terrain at human scale to draw; and the material is one flat colour until P7's class tiles. Real geometry with no material reads as worse than the analytic ground it replaced at close range — expected, and recorded so it is not mistaken for a regression. The punch list is P2c.
- 2026-08-02 — **A vehicle can be put in a scene and driven.** P7 built a drivable vehicle and proved it against solvers the tests constructed themselves; what it could not do was reach a *scene*. §5.5's `VehicleInstance` closes that: a component names a `.sushinodebeam`, `IVehicleService` instances it into the scene's own solver, the drivetrain is spent each tick between the contact submission and the solve, and the rigid core's solved pose is written back onto the entity's Transform.
  - The component stores a **path**, unlike every other asset reference at this boundary, and the difference is deliberate: a soft body's blob is loaded by whoever cooked it and handed across as bytes, while a vehicle is placed by an author in a scene file that has to survive being reopened on another machine — and a path is the only thing that survives that.
  - `set_vehicles` is a **rebuild**, not a diff like `set_rigid_bodies`, and says so: a vehicle is four hundred bodies and two thousand beams placed relative to a cooked structure, so "the same vehicle with one number changed" is not a thing that can be patched in place, and pretending otherwise would leave half a car built to one setup and half to another.
  - The tick order is forced rather than chosen. The tyre model reads its normal load off *this* tick's manifolds (§11.5), so it cannot run before contacts are submitted; it puts velocity impulses on the wheels, so it cannot run after they are solved. Between the two is the only place it fits. The structure's tick boundary — beams denting, mounts tearing out, a part reported as having come off — runs after the solve beside the joint breaking, for §6.6's reason: a topology change never happens against a running graph.
  - Input is **held, not consumed**. Throttle is a state an input device holds down, so a caller that stops calling leaves the pedal where it was — which is what a pedal does, and what lets a UI slider and an input action drive the same car without fighting over whose turn it is.
  - The Vehicle window gained a Scene tab: put this setup on the selected entity, drive it, read the drivetrain back, and see the shell as a side elevation — which is where a sagging suspension and a caved panel are both visible. The rest of the window still edits a document, and that split is honest: the corners and the drivetrain are the same numbers whether or not anything is placed.
- 2026-08-02 — **Surface materials became real, which found a bug they had been hiding.** §5.3's `PhysicsMaterial` has existed since P0 with friction, restitution and four combine modes, and `submit_contacts` was solving *every contact in the world* at a hard-coded 0.6 / 0.5 / 0 regardless — so an ice cube and a rubber block behaved identically and always had. The material is now carried per body on `RigidBodyDesc`, resolved per pair through `make_contact_params`, and authored on the Collider, because that is where a surface is: two crates of the same mesh can be ice and rubber, and nothing about them is shared but the shape.
  - Carried by value rather than as an index into a scene table, exactly as `SoftBodyDesc` already carries its constitutive material. `RigidBodyT::material_index` exists so a *device* kernel can reach a material without a pointer, and the manifold pass that resolves a contact's surfaces runs on the host with the body's own record in hand — a table would be a second place a material could live.
  - A side with no rigid body (the standing plane, a cloth particle) resolves to the default surface, deliberately a fixed ground rather than a mirror of whatever is standing on it: an ice cube should be slippery *against the floor*, and a floor that copied the cube's friction would cancel exactly the difference the author authored.

It cannot be rebuilt outside: the solver is the authority on which generation a slot is on, and a caller that guessed would address a body already recycled.
  - **A wheel must not also have Coulomb friction**, because the solver's own friction runs on the same contact inside the substep loop and the two add. `SuspensionSetupT::material_index` exists so a wheel can point at a frictionless material, and it is the one mistake the tyre cannot detect for itself.
  - One patch per wheel rather than one per manifold point: slip is a property of the patch, and evaluating the curve per point would saturate each separately and give a wheel across a kerb edge more total grip than one flat on the road. The reaction is shared back by each contact's load share, so the impulse the wheel gains is exactly the impulse the world loses — measured as 300.000000000 unchanged across a wheel sliding on a movable floor.
  - Found while testing, and recorded rather than fixed: §7.3's manifold refresh anchors a contact to a *material point*, so a fast-spinning round body carries that point round its rim within a tick and the bottom of the wheel stops being where the contact is. - 2026-08-01 — Added the powertrain chain (P7-G, `docs/slop/physics_system.md` §16.27), §11.4 and §10.5's first escape hatch applied: engine torque curve and inertia → clutch → gearbox → differential → the driven wheels. `PowertrainT` is a one-dimensional multibody system that knows nothing about bodies, handles or solvers — it is handed each driven wheel's spin rate and inertia and hands back a torque — and `VehicleInstanceT::begin_tick` is the whole of the three-dimensional half.
  - **The state is one number.** Gearbox and final drive are exact ratios and the differential's outputs *are* the wheels, so everything downstream of the clutch has its speed determined by speeds the caller measured. The crankshaft is the only free rotational coordinate. A shaft member per stage would have been a cache of derived values, stale the moment anything else moved a wheel.
  - **The clutch is solved, not damped.** The torque that would make both sides equal at the end of the step is computed in closed form and then clamped to the plate's capacity: below capacity it locks *exactly*, above it slips at exactly its rating. A stiff spring between a flywheel and a car in first gear is the explicit integration §0.2 exists to avoid, and it is the classic reason a drivetrain needs a higher rate than the car around it. Measured: after twenty ticks the crank and the geared wheel speed agree to 1e-6 rad/s.
  - **The differential is one number, not three kinds.** `lock_torque` at zero is open, large is a spool, between is a limited-slip — one clamp instead of a branch and a `GearJoint` the solver does not have (§10.2 deferred it here). Measured: open and locked both sum to the shaft torque to 1e-9 N·m, and the locked case gives the slower wheel 1085 N·m against the faster one's 599.
  - **The gearbox's own shafts take their share.** They are geared to the wheels, so they accelerate with them: referred to the wheels they weigh `inertia × ratio²`, which in first gear is comparable to the wheels themselves. Delivering the full shaft torque *and* charging the clutch solve for that inertia would have left the two halves of the chain disagreeing about how fast the driveline was turning, and the clutch would never quite have locked — which is exactly how the defect showed up.
  - **Both wheels of an axle now point their axles the same way.** `SuspensionSetupT::axle` used to be documented as pointing outboard; a wheel is symmetric about its axle so nothing physical asked for that, and it meant the two wheels of one axle turned in opposite senses rolling forward. §11.4 reads one signed speed per wheel and writes one signed torque back, so such a car handed its differential a mean of zero and its chassis two reactions that cancelled exactly. Caught by the reaction test reading zero.
  - **The reaction lands on the chassis, not the carrier.** A driven wheel is turned by a shaft from the differential and the differential's casing is bolted to the chassis, so squat under power is a sprung reaction. Summing the wheel impulses and negating them on the core also means a vehicle's own engine cannot change its total angular momentum. Measured: the core takes -13.402212 N·m·s against the wheels' +13.402212, about the axle and about nothing else.
  - The engine has a torque curve rather than a peak-torque number, because the shape is the engine's character and one number makes every engine an electric motor. Idle is a proportional governor over an authored band, so its droop is the author's number and not a surprise; the limiter is a throttle cut, and its one-tick overshoot on a 0.22 kg·m² crank is real rather than a defect.
  - Reverse is a negative ratio and neutral is a ratio of exactly zero, both in the one ordered list a driver moves through — so selecting a gear is an index and never a mode plus an index.
  - A vehicle whose drivetrain `configure` would refuse is a trailer, not a failure: it instances, drives nothing, and ignores the throttle.

### Fixed
- 2026-08-01 — Fixed soft-body contacts being generated only for surfaces already touching, so a body moving faster than its own thickness per tick passed through whatever it was about to land on — a cube dropped on a cube ended 4.07 m below where it should have rested. The broad phase was already inflating by the tick's travel and the narrow phase then rejected every pair it offered for not touching *yet*; contacts are found once per tick by design (§6.1), and a set built once per tick has to cover the whole tick. Now speculative: a pair is accepted within `thickness + travel` while its `rest_distance` stays at `thickness`, so the extra contacts cost list length and never a spurious push. `ISoftBodyCollider::generate_contacts` takes the tick duration for this reason. Same fix for §9.6.3's self-collision, whose falling half passed through its pinned half for the same reason.
- 2026-08-01 — Fixed a body marked `continuous` tunnelling through geometry the same body would have hit with the flag off: the swept pass replaced the tick's contact set each substep instead of adding to it. It now adds, so enabling the flag can only ever find more.
- 2026-08-01 — Fixed cooked binding weights being applied to absolute positions without renormalizing. `SoftBodyBinding` stores `float` weights, which sum to one only to about six digits after the round trip, displacing a reconstructed point by `|position| × 1e-7` — micrometres near the origin, centimetres at planet scale, and invisible to any test that places its body at the origin. `Cooking::read_binding_weights` renormalizes at the one point every reader goes through.
- 2026-08-01 — Fixed §9.5's fracture leaving two pieces welded at whatever vertices they still shared (P6-G). The gap was recorded as needing cooked face-adjacency data; it does not — a vertex's star is small, and two of its elements are on the same side of a crack exactly when they share three vertices including it, which the vertex lists already answer. Fracture now duplicates such vertices, divides their mass rather than copying it, keeps a pinned vertex pinned in every copy, rebuilds the boundary from the surviving elements, and reports an index remap so the render binding follows (§8.6 invariant 4). The copies start coincident, so the split itself moves nothing.
- 2026-08-01 — Fixed `apply_fem_fracture` being declared `noexcept` while building several vectors, which would have turned an allocation failure into a termination.
- 2026-08-01 — Fixed the soft-body distance field being sampled at nearest-voxel only, which settled a deformable surface onto a staircase and — worse — returned a normal unrelated to the surface, because a half-voxel central difference inside one voxel reads exactly zero and fell through to `sdf_gradient_world`'s fixed-axis guard. Added a trilinear sampler with an analytic gradient beside it; the convex-pair narrowphase path is untouched.
- 2026-08-01 — Fixed the nest's longwave budget being one-way, which cooled a column without bound wherever cloud persisted: measured over 72 h under a fixed sun and a quiescent parent, the deck level at 1585 m reached −42.7 K and the ground −21.7 K, both still falling linearly, while the sky field froze to four decimals from 43 h. Two independent halves — a cloud-top loss that did not depend on the cloud's own temperature, and a ground that radiated to Brutsaert's clear sky while the same deck shaded its sunlight away.
  - Isolated by running the same 72 h with the cloud-top term off: the surface holds +4.5 to +5.8 K across the whole run instead, at the same 0.84 cloud cover.
  - Verified on the same 72 h: net radiation goes −76.3 → **+1.3 W/m²** and the sensible flux −57.7 → −1.6, so the ground is in balance under an overcast noon sky; the surface settles at −1.4 K against −21.7, and the deck at −13.9 K against its 15 K floor rather than falling without end.

- 2026-07-31 — Fixed `humidity_scale_height` folding the base state's *relative humidity* rather than its mixing ratio, which decayed the profile twice and left the airmass far drier than documented — 41 % relative humidity at 1.3 km and 9.5 % at 5 km, against 62 % and 50 % now.
  - Changed the Meteorology panel's land-cover presets to set the airmass humidity along with the surface properties, because a semi-desert is not merely a dry surface: under a 70 % airmass a dry surface still built a 2 km afternoon deck. Each preset now delivers the sky its tooltip describes, measured over 11 h from sunrise.
