- 2026-07-29 — **T0 has real data** (atmosphere phase C, tasks #23–#25): a `Climatology` seam in `sushi_atmosphere`, a baked `SET0` asset, and `se climatology bake` to produce it from public sources. The seven analytic mean-state numbers moved off `QuasiGeostrophicParameters` into `AnalyticClimatology` — they are not parameters *of the dynamics*, they are the cheapest possible statement of a mean state, standing where a real climatology stands, and keeping them in the physics struct made "what the atmosphere relaxes toward" indistinguishable from "how it relaxes". The analytic path is bit-for-bit what stood inline, so every number C2 and C3 measured still holds, and it remains the real path for a non-Earth body rather than a fallback that fails.
  - **ERA5 became NCEP-NCAR Reanalysis 1, deliberately.** ERA5 is behind the Copernicus CDS and needs an account; an asset nobody else can reproduce is an asset nobody else can check. NCEP R1's monthly long-term means are public HTTP at NOAA PSL, so the bake downloads and *computes* rather than trusting numbers typed in from a published figure. The cost is 2.5° against 0.25°, which is irrelevant: every field taken from it is a zonal mean and is one-dimensional by the time it reaches the asset. Measured against textbook climatology — upper jets at 24.8 m/s / 37°N and 27.7 m/s / 49°S, the 30°N jet at **40.2 m/s in January against 4.0 in July**, trade easterlies at 12.5°N.
  - **"Precipitable water" became "saturated column water", because the core wanted a ceiling.** The moisture scheme evaporates toward a fraction of saturation and condenses above it, so observed precipitable water dropped into that slot would be a ceiling about 30 % too low everywhere and it would rain continuously. The bake integrates saturation specific humidity (Tetens, over liquid) across the reanalysis' own temperature profile instead. Observed water is still downloaded and still never written: it is divided by the derived saturation to report the implied column relative humidity, **0.58 in the tropics dipping to 0.42 in the subtropics** — the descending branch of the Hadley cell, reproduced by a derivation never told it exists.
  - **The surface fields ship at 1°, not the 5 km §4 used to promise.** That figure assumed T0's mask told T2 where the shoreline is; it cannot — a nest cell is 2 km over a 384 km footprint, so a raster fine enough would be 200 M cells, and the scene's own terrain already carries that coastline at metre resolution. 1° is also OISST's native grid, so the SST is stored with no reduction and no interpolation error at all. Land is a true *area fraction* (Natural Earth 1:50m vectors, 64 samples per cell) so the subgrid coastline survives as information rather than rounding to land. This is a bake-tool default, not a format constraint: the surface grid is a header field and `adopt()` validates whatever arrives.
  - **The audit is the deliverable, not the file.** A climatology is data nobody eyeballs, from sources nobody re-downloads, feeding a mean state that decides whether the world has weather. So the bake refuses to write unless four things agree: the area-weighted land total against the Earth's known 0.292 (which is what catches non-disjoint polygons silently cancelling to water in the even-odd fill), the implied column humidity, the polar sea-surface fill, and a full round trip of the written bytes. Attribution and SHA-256 of every input travel *inside* the asset, so they cannot be separated from the data; `se climatology inspect` prints them.
  - Two defects the audit caught and fixed. An all-land latitude row — 80°S spans Antarctica at every longitude — took its fill from the global ocean mean and wrote **287 K under the ice sheet**; it now interpolates from the nearest latitude that has ocean, giving 271.5 K. And NCEP's own precipitable water goes slightly negative over the Antarctic plateau, which made the humidity cross-check report a *negative* relative humidity; the check is now reported where it can be read and the artefact is named rather than hidden. Verified across the language seam: the C++ `adopt()` reads what Python wrote, places both jets, keeps December→January continuous to 0.0012 m/s, answers land correctly at six probe points from the Sahara to Antarctica, and **refuses a truncated blob while leaving the already-loaded climatology working and unchanged**.
  - `netCDF4`/`numpy`/`requests` are an optional extra (`pip install -e cli[climatology]`), not a CLI dependency: the bake is a rare developer step whose output is committed, and `se build` should not drag HDF5 in to compile the engine.
- 2026-07-29 — **The global core now runs on the real climatology** (atmosphere phase C, task #26). `Simulation::load_climatology` is the one place in the engine that names the asset, and it is on the simulation side because `sushi_atmosphere` links nothing — `Climatology` adopts bytes and deliberately cannot open a file, so the same object still serves the editor, a headless probe and a unit test. `ProceduralWeather` takes the mean state by value rather than reading a path itself: which climatology the dynamics run on is the caller's decision, and a test wants analytic bands where the editor wants the asset. Both paths verified end to end — the provider reports `baked()` true with the asset present and falls back to analytic bands without it, with no error to check, because §4's analytic degradation is a working mean state and not a failure mode.
  - **Measured, 512×256 over 60 days, against the same probe on analytic bands:** eddy energy growth **0.2098/day** (analytic: 0.2600), e-folding 9.53 days, peak shear 34 m/s at 29°N, zonal KE ~1.0 × 10⁶ J/m², and a complete baroclinic life cycle — growth through day 38, saturation at day 48, decay, second cycle from day 58. Global mean rain reaches **1.5–2.4 mm/day against the analytic state's 0.235**, an order of magnitude closer to the observed ~2.7. The core reports its jet at **29–31°N**: nothing tells it where to put a jet, and that is where January's subtropical jet is in the reanalysis it relaxes toward.
  - **No retuning was needed, which was not the expectation.** A mean state with nearly twice the shear was predicted to grow much faster and push `grid_scale_damping_seconds` off its calibration. It grows *slower*, because the real jet is narrow and sits at 29°N rather than broad at 45°, where β is larger and the Phillips threshold it must clear is higher. The parameter is untouched.
  - **Fixed the probe's growth-rate fit window, which reported `0.0000 /day over 1 samples`.** It opened at a fixed day 2, which held only because the analytic mean state happened to be growing by then; a real climatology is not smooth in latitude, so the seed spends its first days shedding the part no mode wants, energy *falls* first, and the window closed one sample later. A fall is now read as "still shaking down" and restarts the window until enough growth has accumulated that a fall can only be the peak. The analytic case is unchanged at **0.2600/day**, matching C2's independent 0.26. Also gave the probe `--climatology PATH` (refusing rather than silently falling back, since a sweep that quietly ran analytic bands would report a growth rate for a mean state nobody asked for) and `--damping-hours`.
  - Fixed a latent build break: `global_core_main.cpp` used `AnalyticClimatology` and `Climatology` without using-declarations after the phase-C seam landed, so the probe target had not compiled since.
  - **Named limit: the moisture cycles too slowly, and the ceiling is not what is wrong.** Column water settles at 31.3 kg/m² against an observed ~25 while rain runs low, so the two miss in *opposite* directions and no value of `evaporation_relative_humidity` fixes both. That pattern indicates the condensation and evaporation timescales rather than the saturation profile, so the parameter is left at 0.75 deliberately instead of moved to the observed 0.55 because one number would have matched.
- 2026-07-29 — **The world has seasons** (atmosphere phase C, task #27). `ProceduralWeather::tick` was dropping its `julian_date`; it now converts it to a position in the year and moves the mean state before advancing the flow into it, so a step always runs against the climatology of the moment it belongs to. `QuasiGeostrophicCore::set_year_fraction` re-reads exactly two things — the saturation ceiling and the climatological state — and touches nothing prognostic: a season is not a force on the flow, it is a change to what the flow is relaxing toward, so a cyclone alive in April finds itself in a slightly different mean flow rather than vanishing.
  - **Determinism is preserved by quantizing, not by thresholding.** The obvious way to avoid rebuilding two tables every frame is "rebuild once the date has moved enough", and it is wrong: it makes the mean state depend on the call history, so two hosts ticking at different rates diverge. The year fraction is quantized to whole days instead — a pure function of the date. T0 holds twelve monthly fields, so a day oversamples the data thirty to one and nothing is lost, while the rebuild happens ~365 times a simulated year rather than 60 times a second. Measured: a host ticking 1000 times and one ticking 10 times across the same interval land on mean states identical to **nine decimal places**.
  - The epoch is applied **before** the seed, so a scene opening in July is seeded with July's jet instead of spending its first simulated weeks migrating out of January's — a transient nobody asked for that would read as the weather being wrong.
  - Measured through the provider at each solstice: 35°N upper wind **34.55 m/s in January against 11.37 in July**; 30°S **16.45 against 35.97**. And the Southern Hemisphere jet peak moves from **48°S in January to 29°S in July** while the northern one merely weakens — the SH double-jet structure (one merged eddy-driven jet near 50°S in summer, splitting in winter so the strongest flow is the subtropical jet at 30°S). Nothing in the engine knows the hemispheres differ; it falls out of the data. It cost a wrong assertion to find: a check written at 45°S failed because 45°S is exactly the crossover between the two jets and says nothing either way.
  - Added `Astro::calendar_from_julian_date`, the exact inverse of the existing `julian_date_from_calendar` (Fliegel-Van Flandern reversal, valid across the proleptic Gregorian calendar), and `Simulation::year_fraction_from_julian_date`. The conversion lives on the simulation side because `sushi_atmosphere` is engine-neutral: the core speaks in a year fraction, which is a position in an abstract cycle that works for any body with any period, and a Julian Date is a fact about Earth's civil calendar. The fraction is built from the month index plus the fraction elapsed *within that month* rather than from the day of the year, because T0's twelve fields are sampled as twelve equal bins and calendar months are not equal — a day-of-year fraction would drift up to a day and a half out of phase, worst in the months whose climatology changes fastest.
- 2026-07-29 — **Phase C is closed** (atmosphere, task #29). The Meteorology panel gained a read-only **Mean State (T0)** section: whether the running climatology is the baked asset or the analytic latitude bands, the season the scene is at, where the jet is and how much vertical shear it carries — with a warning when that shear falls below the Phillips threshold, because a mean state that will never grow a storm is otherwise indistinguishable from one that has not grown one *yet*. The attribution baked into the asset is shown beside the weather it produced. Nothing here is authorable, deliberately — T0 is data, and a slider over a climatology would be a slider over a measurement.
  - `IWeatherProvider` gained `climatology()`, returning null by default. That is the honest answer for most providers rather than a stub: a static sky and an ingested METAR feed are not departures from a climatology, they *are* the weather, given rather than grown.

- 2026-07-29 — **The global dynamical core is now the weather** (atmosphere phase C3): `SynopticLayer` — eight authored pressure-system ellipses with headings, lifecycle timers and drawn front rays — is deleted, and T1 drives the regional nest, the editor, the gameplay wind and the scene file. Re-measured through the shipped provider with **nothing injected**, the core grows eddies exponentially at **0.254/day over days 8–20** (matching C2's independent 0.26/day), draws the zonal flow down from 704 000 to 466 000 J/m² and hands it back, peaks at 42 m/s and 0.235 mm/day of rain, then decays — a baroclinic life cycle, unscripted. A `FrontPassage` preset produces a **measured front passage** over the observer: the anomaly deepens overhead, passes, the ridge builds behind it (−90 → +29 hPa), and the wind **veers from north-northeast to nearly due west** while the thermal gradient sharpens from 1.02 to 1.29 K/100 km.
  - Retargeted the Davies forcing onto real fields. Three interim constructions went out with the analytic layer: a stylized warm/cold sector mask driving the anomalies, the four constants describing how strong that sector was, and C1's Ekman-pumping estimate of the vertical motion — the core supplies all three directly, the last as its quasi-geostrophic omega. The two anomaly fields are departures **from the zonal mean**, because the nest's base state already carries the mean and what it cannot generate is the eddy; `frontal_strength_at` is the magnitude of the **total** thermal gradient, because removing the mean first would erase the background baroclinic zone that frontogenesis concentrates.
  - **Corrected `pressure_anomaly_hpa`, which returned the total rather than an anomaly.** It reported `rho f0 psi_2`, dominated by the mean jet's own meridional gradient — measured at **139 hPa across a 40-degree window**, an order more than any cyclone — so the editor's map would have been a picture of latitude. Now a departure from the zonal mean like its two siblings, with `QuasiGeostrophicDiagnostics::lowest_pressure_anomaly_hpa` corrected alongside it so "the deepest low in the world" names a low rather than whichever cell sits furthest poleward.
  - Reshaped authoring around what a dynamical core can honour: `IWeatherAuthoring` loses `synoptic()` and gains `inject_vorticity` — a low is a feature of a field, not an object, and what an author can do to a field is disturb it. The disturbance then deepens, tilts, sheds a front and moves with the steering flow instead of translating as a rigid shape. Presets became initial conditions rather than scripts. The read-side queries moved to `IWeatherProvider`, where every provider answers honestly, and `ISimulation` gained a const `weather_provider()` so the editor's map can sample the field without being able to disturb it.
  - **The editor's synoptic map samples a field instead of drawing a list.** It shades `pressure_anomaly_hpa` or `frontal_strength_at` on a 44×44 lattice with a 9×9 wind-arrow field over it, normalized against the extremum actually present and reporting that number, and injects an anomaly where clicked. Every slider the old panel offered — depth, radius, speed, heading — set a quantity the core now derives, and a slider that pretends to set a derived quantity is worse than no slider.
  - Scene persistence moved to a **binary sidecar** beside the scene JSON. The state is two potential-vorticity fields and a moisture field on a 512×256 grid — **1 572 920 bytes measured** — which is not going into a human-editable text file for a payload no human would edit. `q − f` is stored as float so all seven digits go to the varying part; a round trip was measured at 3×10⁻⁸ hPa, a junk blob is rejected, and a missing or wrong-grid sidecar leaves the core as seeded rather than failing the whole scene load.
  - Retired the hemisphere defect with the class that carried it: `SynopticLayer::wind_at` applied the surface-friction turn as the same rotation in both hemispheres, so every southern-hemisphere boundary wind the nest was ever given was turned the wrong way. The core turns it correctly and a unit test pins both signs.
  - Confirmed rather than fixed `Astro::Ephemeris` as the single solar authority: the nest already reads the sine of the *rendered* sun's elevation off the environment the ephemeris fills, and no second solar model survives anywhere in the simulation.
  - Named limit: the preset and click amplitudes are about 3× too strong — the editor's defaults (700 km, 20 m/s) put −70.6 hPa on the map, `FrontPassage` −94.8, `Storm` −110.9, where a deep real low is −30. `GAUSSIAN_BLOB_WIND_FACTOR` is the correct constant and the field is geostrophically self-consistent; what makes it deep is that `inject_vorticity` places a *monopole*, whose streamfunction grows logarithmically inward instead of decaying the way a compensated anomaly's does. Recorded as open — a compensated blob or simply smaller amplitudes — rather than guessed at.
  - Withdrew a wrong claim from the entry this replaced: it also said a naturally mature cyclone reads only −6.9 hPa against a textbook −31, implying the dynamics were as mis-scaled as the injection. That was measured on the default 30 m/s jet, which sits barely above the Phillips threshold. On a properly supercritical jet the core reaches **−35.4 hPa by day 8**, with the eddy energy peaking and the zonal flow drawn down — a textbook deep low. Only the injection is loud.
  - Split `TheJetsPressureAnomalyIsSynopticAndNotAstronomical`, which the corrected reference broke and which had not been re-run: a purely zonal state now asserts *exactly* zero anomaly (the claim the old total-field code failed, by 139 hPa), and the synoptic-amplitude bound moved onto a flow that has actually gone unstable.
  - Recorded a trap that cost real time: `advance()` is capped at `max_steps_per_advance` (8), so **one call can never cover more than 48 minutes of game time**. A first measurement handed it two-day intervals in a loop and concluded the core barely grew and never rained — it had simulated ten hours, not 24 days. Drive it against `simulated_seconds()`.
- 2026-07-29 — Added `sushi_atmosphere`, the engine-neutral atmosphere module, and in it **T1, the global dynamical core** (atmosphere phase C2): two-layer moist quasi-geostrophic flow on a 512×256 latitude/longitude grid, with potential vorticity and column water as the only prognostic variables and everything else — wind, pressure, vertical motion, fronts, the jet — diagnosed from them. Cyclogenesis is **emergent**: the initial state is a zonal jet plus a perturbation of a hundredth of its speed, and what grows is whatever the mean state is unstable to. Measured over 70 simulated days at full resolution: eddy kinetic energy grows exponentially at 0.26/day (0.32/day over the purely exponential days 8–12), saturates near 1.1×10⁵ J/m² against a 4.2×10⁵ J/m² zonal flow, and then runs a 15–20 day baroclinic life cycle rather than either dying or running away; peak winds reach 49 m/s and cyclones deepen to −31 hPa. One step costs 36 ms of one core and is due once per six minutes of game time.
  - Added the elliptic inversion the core is built on: the barotropic and baroclinic modes decouple **exactly**, so recovering the streamfunction is a Fourier transform in longitude and a pre-factored tridiagonal solve in latitude. The grid is cell-centred, so its polar edges carry `cos(latitude) = 0` and the boundary condition is the one the metric already supplies.
  - Added `FourierTransform`, a radix-2 transform with precomputed tables, including the pairing that rides two real rows on one complex transform — the core's two modes are exactly two, so this halves the transform cost and is not an approximation.
  - Added the quasi-geostrophic omega as a diagnosed field, from the upper layer's own vorticity budget rather than a separate equation. It replaces C1's Ekman-pumping estimate as the large-scale vertical motion the regional nest will be given, and it costs no extra solve.
  - Added `atmosphere_global_probe`, a headless driver that runs simulated weeks in seconds and fits a growth rate to the eddy energy while it is still exponential.
  - Corrected `docs/slop/atmosphere_system.md` §3.3, which planned T1 on the GPU: every consumer of the tier is host-side, so the device would have bought a step that is already nearly free and paid for it with a readback.
- 2026-07-29 — Added large-scale vertical motion to the atmosphere forcing seam (phase C1), the first term the nest receives that is not a boundary condition: it is applied across the whole domain, because a 384 km window has no way to know it is sitting under a thousand-kilometre high. Derived as **Ekman pumping** from the synoptic layer, which already carried everything needed and had nothing reading it for this — its geostrophic wind has a 25° surface-friction turn, and that turn *is* the cross-isobaric flow, so the convergence into a low and the divergence out of a high give ascent and subsidence with no tuning constant. Taken as the divergence of the near-surface wind over the Ekman layer; measured at 1.0 cm/s in the mean and ±5.5 cm/s at the centres of a 20 hPa pair, which is the textbook synoptic range. The nest applies it as vertical advection of potential temperature and vapour, so subsidence warms by compression and dries by bringing down air from where there is less water. `atmosphere_probe --parent-subsidence` measures the term in isolation.
- 2026-07-28 — Added the surface energy balance, cloud shading and ice to the Meteorology panel, the scene format and `atmosphere_probe` (phase B3e), and ran Phase B's acceptance bar. The panel now authors what a place *is* — albedo, moisture availability, heat capacity, and the mixed-phase band — and reports the fluxes, skin temperature and Bowen ratio the model solved, rather than taking two of them as input. Two of the bar's three clauses are met and emergent: a radiation fog forms overnight because the ground radiates to a cold sky, burns off at mid-morning when the sensible flux turns positive, and a fair-weather cumulus deck forms at the mixed-layer top with a base over a kilometre up. The third — evening decay — is not: the deck grows through the night, and a control run identifies the missing sink as large-scale subsidence, which a limited-area model receives from its parent rather than generating. Carried to Phase C rather than substituted for. Unit tests pin the surface balance's equilibrium and stability, the slant-path insolation, and both saturation curves against textbook values.
- 2026-07-28 — Added ice to the regional atmosphere's microphysics (phase B3d) as a diagnosed phase rather than a second condensate species. A cell's ice fraction ramps from 0 at 0 °C to 1 at −20 °C, and the saturation curve, the latent heat, the autoconversion threshold, the fall speed and the optical effective radius all follow from it — so a cold cloud condenses against the ice curve (well under the liquid one), releases 13 % more heat, precipitates at a quarter the condensate, drops something falling at a metre a second instead of seven, and renders nearly four times thinner for the same water. Measured on one forcing across three airmasses: mean coverage rises 0.430 → 0.621 as the surface cools from 288 K to 265 K *while the condensate falls*, and the cold column holds 3.5× the precipitation per unit cloud. The warm case is bit-identical to before it existed, which is pinned by unit test. - 2026-07-29 — Fixed **an authored low being three times deeper than a natural one, and felt across the hemisphere** (atmosphere, task #21 — the open question C3 named and left). A click at the panel's defaults put **−70.6 hPa** over the map, `FrontPassage` −94.8, `Storm` −110.9, where a deep real cyclone is −30 and the deepest ever recorded is near −50. Nothing was arithmetically wrong: the field was geostrophically self-consistent and 0.451 is the correct peak-azimuthal-wind constant for a Gaussian blob. The cause was that `inject_vorticity` placed a **monopole** — a blob carrying net circulation — whose streamfunction grows *logarithmically outward* instead of decaying, so placing one low tilted the pressure field of the whole hemisphere while still looking like a plausible synoptic pattern.
  - **Fixed as physics rather than as a smaller number.** Every injection now lays down a broader opposing lobe carrying exactly the core's circulation, scaled by summing both lobes over the actual grid rather than from a ratio of analytic integrals — so the cancellation is exact at any latitude, including near the poles where the metric is nothing like a plane. The anomaly 60–120° away fell from *rivalling the centre* to 2–5 hPa.
  - **The amplitude contract was re-derived, not left to drift.** The compensating lobe is a wind of its own opposing the core's, so a pair built on the lone blob's 0.451 delivers a fifth less than asked: a requested 20 m/s blew at 16.2. Summing both lobes gives `0.369 · ζ₀R`, and the requested 20 now measures 19.75. Leaving it would not have been a small error but the parameter quietly meaning something other than its name.
  - **Only then the amplitudes.** With the far field no longer inflating everything, the presets turned out to be asking for 26 and 34 m/s of rotational wind — hurricane force at synoptic radii. Re-measured and re-tuned: editor default **−26.1 hPa** (700 km, 12 m/s), `Overcast` −24.8, `FrontPassage` −34.7, `Storm` −44.1, `Clear` +28.5. The strength slider's ceiling dropped from 45 to 30 m/s, since past about 30 it was offering an author a system the atmosphere has never made.
  - Four new `Unit_QuasiGeostrophicCore` cases pin it: the anomaly is local, it blows at the speed it was asked for, it is a depth that could exist, and its sign is right in both directions.

- 2026-07-28 — Fixed the atmosphere nest's time step being pinned three times shorter than anything the model needed. It added a fresh perturbation to potential temperature every step, so across 37 000 columns its cold tail kept a few percent of the domain in permanent ground fog which the sky then reported as cloud. It now modulates the surface heat flux, which is where surface heterogeneity physically lives: bounded by construction, and it stops at dusk with the flux it scales. `thermal_seed_amplitude` becomes a dimensionless patchiness (editor: *Surface Patchiness*) rather than a rate in K/s.
- 2026-07-28 — Fixed a level that is momentarily colder than the one above it decoupling from the atmosphere nest's boundary layer entirely: the parcel test correctly found the column stable, diagnosed zero mixing depth, and left the level exchanging with nothing — so the thermal seed's random walk accumulated in it without limit, measured at −8.76 K after four hours at 99 % relative humidity while the level above sat at 50 %. The diagnosed depth now carries a floor of the two lowest levels, which is the surface layer's mechanical turbulence and does not switch off with the stratification. Every cloud the nest made before this was that fog.
- 2026-07-28 — Fixed the cloudscape bake eroding the nest path's *density* instead of its *shape*, which turned any thin deck into flickering specks. The fringe erosion subtracts an absolute threshold averaging 0.225, written for the deck path where density is an authored quantity of order one; the nest path's density is measured, and a marginal fair-weather deck (14 % cloud fraction, 0.01 g/kg, sitting at the closure's critical humidity) arrives at 0.058 — so the erosion drove 87 % of its texels to zero and left the noise's own peaks, which changed every rebake. The carve and the erosion now run on the dimensionless shape and the measured water multiplies once at the end.
